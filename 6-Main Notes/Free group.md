# Free group
A group is called a free group if no relation exists between its group generators other than  the relationship between an element and its inverse required as one of the defining properties of a [group](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/Group.html).

The dimension (rank) of a free group is the cardinality of its generating set.

In other words, given a set $S$, the free group $F_{S}$ over $S$ consists of all words that can be built from members of $S$, considering two words to be different unless their equality follows from the group axioms (eg. $st = suu^{-1}t \text{ but } s \neq t^{-1} \text{ for } s,t,u \in S$). The members of $S$ are called the generators of $F_{S}$, and the number of generators is called the rank of the free group. An arbitrary group $G$ is free if it is isomorphic to $F_{S}$ for some $S \subset G$, that is, if there is a subset $S$ of $G$ such that every element of $G$ can be written in exactly one way as a product of finitely many elements of $S$ and their inverses (disregarding trivial variations such as $st = suu^{-1}t$).
