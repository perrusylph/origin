## Remark 

When the topological space $M$ is connected, the integer $n$ in the definition does not depend on the chart and is the _dimension_ of the manifold $M$. 
## Exercise (After Remark on Definition 1.8)

Check the same statement for smooth maps.
The statement would be:
_If $U \subset \mathbf{R}^{n}$ is open and $f:U \to \mathbf{R}^{n}$ is one-one and smooth, then $f(U) \subset \mathbf{R}^{n}$ is open. 

## Proof

Not easy to do because we can't use IFT, but if the statement refers to the remark, it is easier because we know that the transition map is a diffeomorphism, then the chain rule applied to the transition map and its inverse will tell us that the derivative of the transition map and the derivative of its inverse are inverse, which gives us that the derivative is an isomorphism and that the dimensions are the same. This is inspired by 1.2 in this link: [Lecture 2: Smooth Manifolds](http://staff.ustc.edu.cn/~wangzuoq/Courses/18F-Manifolds/Notes/Lec02.pdf)
